The Heart-Healthy Diet: A Practical Guide to Eating for Your Heart

What to eat more of, what to limit, and simple swaps based on DASH and Mediterranean-style eating — with ideas that work across cuisines.

✍️ HeartConnect Editorial Team📅 Updated 2026-11-18⏱ 8 min read📚 Based on guidance from leading heart organisations

No single "superfood" protects your heart. What matters is your overall eating pattern, repeated day after day. Two of the best-studied patterns — the DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) diet and Mediterranean-style eating — share the same core idea: mostly plants, minimally processed foods, healthy fats, and less salt and sugar.

Eat more of these

  • Vegetables and fruit — aim for half your plate. Fresh, frozen and canned without added salt or sugar all count.
  • Whole grains — oats, brown or red rice, whole-wheat atta, millets (ragi, jowar, bajra), barley, quinoa.
  • Legumes — beans, lentils (dal), chickpeas and peas provide protein and fibre.
  • Nuts and seeds — a small handful of unsalted nuts most days.
  • Healthy oils — olive, canola, mustard, groundnut or sunflower oil in moderate amounts instead of butter, ghee, lard or palm oil.
  • Fish — especially oily fish such as salmon, sardines or mackerel, about twice a week. Vegetarians can include walnuts, flaxseed and chia.
  • Low-fat dairy or unsweetened alternatives such as yoghurt (curd).

Limit these

  • Salt — the WHO recommends less than 5 g of salt (about one teaspoon) per day for adults. Most comes from processed, packaged and restaurant foods, pickles, papads and sauces.
  • Added sugars — sugary drinks, sweets and desserts. Keep free sugars below 10% of daily energy, ideally lower.
  • Processed and red meats — sausages, bacon, salami and frequent fatty red meat.
  • Trans fats — partially hydrogenated oils (vanaspati), some baked and fried snacks.
  • Refined grains — white bread, maida-based snacks and pastries.
  • Alcohol — if you drink, keep it low; less is better for heart health.

Simple swaps that add up

Instead of…Try…
Fried snacksRoasted chana, unsalted nuts, fruit
White rice every mealHalf brown rice or millet, more vegetables
Sugary soft drinksWater with lemon, unsweetened tea, buttermilk without added salt
Salt at the tableHerbs, lemon, pepper, garlic, cumin
Processed meat sandwichBean, egg or grilled chicken wrap with salad
Butter or ghee for cookingSmall amounts of a vegetable oil

A sample heart-healthy day

  • Breakfast: oats or vegetable poha with nuts and fruit, or whole-grain toast with egg and tomato.
  • Lunch: dal or bean curry, whole-grain roti or brown rice, a large salad or sabzi, curd.
  • Snack: fruit and a small handful of nuts.
  • Dinner: grilled fish, tofu or paneer with lots of vegetables and a whole grain.

Reading food labels

Check serving size first, then look at sodium, saturated fat, trans fat and added sugars. A useful rule: 5% Daily Value or less is low; 20% or more is high. Compare similar products and pick the lower-sodium option.

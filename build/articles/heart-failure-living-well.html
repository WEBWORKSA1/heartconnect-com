Heart failure means the heart isn't pumping or filling as well as it should to meet the body's needs. It does not mean the heart has stopped. It is a long-term condition, but with modern treatment and good self-care, many people live active, fulfilling lives for years.

Common symptoms

  • Shortness of breath during activity, when lying flat, or waking up breathless at night
  • Swelling (oedema) in the ankles, legs or abdomen
  • Rapid weight gain from fluid build-up
  • Tiredness and reduced ability to exercise
  • Persistent cough or wheezing, reduced appetite

Types of heart failure

  • Reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF): the heart muscle is weakened and pumps less blood with each beat.
  • Preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF): the heart pumps normally but is stiff and doesn't fill properly.
  • Mildly reduced ejection fraction: somewhere in between.

Ejection fraction is usually measured with an echocardiogram.

Common causes

Coronary artery disease and previous heart attacks, long-standing high blood pressure, diabetes, heart valve disease, cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias such as AFib, heavy alcohol use and some cancer treatments.

Treatments

  • Medicines: several classes of medicines have been shown to help people with heart failure live longer and stay out of hospital. Your team will tailor a combination and adjust doses over time.
  • Devices: some people benefit from an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) or cardiac resynchronisation therapy (CRT).
  • Procedures: treating blocked arteries or faulty valves.
  • Advanced therapies: left ventricular assist devices (LVADs) or heart transplant for selected people.
  • Cardiac rehabilitation: supervised exercise and education.

Daily self-care that makes a real difference

  1. Weigh yourself every morning after using the toilet and before eating. Report rapid gains (your team will give you a threshold, often about 2 kg / 4–5 lb in a few days).
  2. Take medicines every day, even when you feel well.
  3. Limit salt and follow any fluid advice your team gives you.
  4. Stay active within your limits.
  5. Know your warning signs and have an action plan agreed with your care team.
  6. Get vaccinated against flu and other infections as recommended.
Call emergency services for severe breathlessness at rest, chest pain, fainting or coughing up pink, frothy mucus.

Heart disease is one of the leading causes of death for women worldwide, yet many women still think of it as a "man's disease". Women's symptoms are more likely to be dismissed or misread, and some risk factors are unique to women. Knowing the facts helps you speak up for yourself and the women you love.

Symptoms more common in women

Chest pain or discomfort is still the most common heart attack symptom in women. But women are more likely than men to also experience:

  • Shortness of breath
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Pain in the back, neck, jaw or upper stomach
  • Unusual or extreme fatigue, sometimes for days beforehand
  • Light-headedness or cold sweats

If something feels wrong, trust your instincts and call emergency services. Read our full guide to heart attack warning signs.

Risk factors unique to women

  • Pregnancy complications: high blood pressure in pregnancy, pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes and pre-term birth are linked to higher heart disease risk later in life. Tell your doctor if you've had them.
  • Menopause: after menopause, changes in hormones are associated with changes in cholesterol, blood pressure and body fat distribution.
  • Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS): associated with insulin resistance and other risk factors.
  • Autoimmune diseases: such as lupus and rheumatoid arthritis, which are more common in women.
  • Some breast cancer treatments can affect the heart.

Heart conditions that affect women more

  • Coronary microvascular disease: problems in the heart's tiny blood vessels, which may not show on a standard angiogram.
  • Spontaneous coronary artery dissection (SCAD): a tear in a heart artery that can cause a heart attack, often in younger women with few risk factors.
  • Stress cardiomyopathy ("broken heart syndrome"): a sudden, usually temporary weakening of the heart triggered by intense stress.

What every woman can do

  1. Know your numbers: blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, waist size and BMI. Use our free tools.
  2. Share your full pregnancy history with your doctor.
  3. Move most days and eat a heart-healthy diet.
  4. Don't smoke, and limit alcohol.
  5. Prioritise sleep and manage stress (here's how).
  6. If you're not being heard, ask for a second opinion. Our Care Concierge can help.
